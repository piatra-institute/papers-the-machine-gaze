# Editorial record

Status: full read and revision complete (2026-10-01).

## Contribution and reader

For philosophers of technology and biology, systems engineers and social theorists who use "machine" either as an explanatory category or as a term of critique: a checkable definition of being a machine under a description (closure of the dynamics on the observed variables), the propositions that follow from it, and three computations that show what a machine description leaves out and what happens when a system is acted on or remade through it. Confidence is that of elementary theorems and minimal models, checked numerically; the reproduction of the self-sorting arrays is compared with the published numbers.

## Strongest rival

The organicist position (Nicholson; Canguilhem) holds that organisms differ from machines in kind, so a definition symmetric between organisms and artifacts misses what is distinctive about life. The paper accepts the distinction where it is stated as normativity (a system that can change its own criterion of success leaves any quotient defined by the old one) and shows that the symmetric definition already predicts the failures organicists emphasize; it makes no claim that organisms and artifacts are alike in other respects. A second rival treats the machine critique as purely historical or political; the paper restricts its use of Marx, Taylor, Foucault, Mumford, Scott, Heidegger and Deleuze to the operation each describes.
